**Crucial counterfactual:** Frontier AI progress **stops on September 1, 2026**. Models do not become substantially more capable or superhuman; hardware gets cheaper/more abundant. By around 2040 every person can access the compute equivalent of a contemporary B300 GPU for a 2026-class frontier model, 24/7. The essay must explore that *fixed-capability, radically abundant-compute* world. A singularity, recursive superintelligence, improved foundation-model capability, or 'AI gets smarter every year' story fails the stated prompt.

## Specific entry concept / author revision instructions

**Working angle (not entry text):** *The Last Expensive Thing Is a Second Opinion.* The year is 2040. The same fallible 2026-class model is available to nearly everyone, nearly all the time. Computation is abundant; independent verification, accountability, skilled dissent, shared facts and human learning remain scarce. Distinguish access to answers from the ability to judge answers.

**Suggested 2,500–3,200-word nonfiction scenario structure, for the human author to compose:**
1. 2040 concrete scene: a teenager with effectively unlimited personalized tutoring, but an unassisted examination reveals what was not learned (~350 words).
2. State the *frozen-capability* assumption precisely; no new model intelligence, only compute availability and changed distribution (~300).
3. Three consequences of abundance: individualized tutoring, cheap local-language assistance, and infinitely personalized public explanations; show gains as well as costs (~750).
4. The remaining bottleneck: provenance, trust, institutional accountability, apprenticeship and verification; distinguish 2026 evidence from speculative 2040 projections (~800).
5. Design choices and tradeoffs: unplugged assessments, source-verifiable claims, shared public reference points, human appeal paths; one non-obvious ending (~500).
6. Trim to 1,000–5,000 words; verify every factual claim and remove any assumption of post-2026 capability breakthroughs.
