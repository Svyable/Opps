## Economics and decision threshold — not a win-rate claim

Lot C's official ceiling is **EUR 10,000 excl. VAT**. Assume a **21-day** delivery workload across 14 months (14 monitoring days + 5 mapping/review days + 2 reporting days) and **EUR 350/day** alternative labor value. Conditional economic contribution at the ceiling would be **EUR 2,650** before tax, payment delay, insurance if later needed, legal costs, FX and possible termination. At 30 days, the same model yields **EUR -500**, so fixed-price scope creep is material.

A complete tender would take an estimated **6 hours × EUR 35/hour = EUR 210**, *if* CVs, two references and sample rights are already ready. No historical award odds are published. Illustrative full-pursuit scenarios:

| Scenario | P(qualified/capacity) | P(award given qualified) | Net benefit if performed | EV = product − EUR 210 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Cautious | 20% | 3% | EUR 0 | **EUR -210** |
| Central | 40% | 8% | EUR 2,650 | **EUR -125.20** |
| Favorable | 70% | 15% | EUR 4,750 (15-day delivery at EUR 350/day) | **EUR +288.75** |

These probabilities and delivery-day estimates are **subjective sensitivity assumptions**, not observed Eticas bid rates. EUR 45k is the aggregate of four lots and must not be counted as Lot C's value. A modest **one-hour/EUR 35** preflight can be useful to avoid a six-hour disqualified tender; it does **not** establish a positive full-bid EV or improve the probability of award.

**Release gates before a full tender:** (1) truthful demonstrable CEN-CENELEC/ISO knowledge and availability through June 2028; (2) two reachable references, dated CV and 2–3 rights-cleared samples; (3) bounded effort estimate and approved EUR price; (4) explicit acceptance decision on 90-day offer, IP assignment, termination, delayed payments, penalty, Spanish jurisdiction and tax; (5) written applicant approval. If any fail, keep WATCH and do not submit. **No legal commitment or contact was made.**
