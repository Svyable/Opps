## Distinct pilot: 'Who authorized the agent?'

Proposed **three original, source-audited explanatory investigations**, each with a documented falsification test and a rights-cleared public release. No investigation or interview has been completed.

1. **The instruction was not permission.** Explain the difference between an AI agent being instructed not to act and an external system actually preventing tool execution. Ground technical demonstrations in Svyable's [synthetic gateway](https://github.com/Svyable/Opps/blob/main/artifacts/control-evidence-2026/mock_gateway.py) and [receipt auditor](https://github.com/Svyable/Opps/blob/main/artifacts/control-evidence-2026/receipt_audit.py), labeling them synthetic. Report independently verifiable real-world cases only after locating primary incident records and requesting responses where necessary. **Disconfirming evidence:** a proposed example has no independent executor evidence; remove or qualify it.
2. **Who gets to say yes?** Examine authorization chains for agents using enterprise tools: user intent, service account permission, organizational policy, and actual executor outcome. Make a concrete case-study table linking each claimed control to a primary technical document, reproducible demo, or independently authenticated event. **Disconfirming evidence:** a vendor policy promise without an observable enforcement boundary is not a measured safety outcome.
3. **The audit trail that isn't there.** Reproduce how missing, duplicate, orphan, and contradictory receipts can make an evaluation misleading; publish synthetic negative controls and code hashes, clearly separating reproducible validator tests from agent deployment results. **Disconfirming evidence:** a receipt originated from the same simulated policy component; it does not establish independent execution.

**Format hypothesis:** three 1,500–2,500-word articles, optionally adapted into short videos using existing production capability. Neither production skill nor audience traction is assumed. Each piece needs primary-source URLs, a claim ledger with evidence strength, a correction mechanism, and documented rights for illustrations and quotations. Avoid republishing a book chapter as original journalism.

## Reviewer-facing evidence gates

- **Craft:** provide 2–3 actual public samples, with dated URLs and original reporting or rigorous explanatory work; book metadata alone is insufficient.
- **Accuracy:** deliver a claim-by-claim source ledger; independent technical reviewer or documented verification procedure; disclose synthetic versus real-world results.
- **Audience:** attach dated platform analytics (unique reach, completion/retention, newsletter or reader metrics) and a distribution experiment. **No analytics verified.**
- **Execution:** identify author/editor capacity, jurisdiction, legal payee, tax treatment, publication schedule and original work rights.
- **Budget:** obtain quotes or real time/cost records; isolate all media expenses from TAIF's Control Evidence research and BlueDot Rapid. **Verified exclusive expenses: $0 (unknown, not free).**
- **Terms:** inspect actual award agreement, disclosure, IP, payment, cancellation and return-of-funds clauses; obtain applicant approval before applying.
