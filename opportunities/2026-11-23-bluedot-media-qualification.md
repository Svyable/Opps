## Funding / conditions
$20,000–$200,000 over 6–12 months; applications accepted on a rolling basis (no fixed closing date identified); about four weeks for a decision. BlueDot states it supports books, long-form writing, videos, podcasts and journalism about advanced-AI and biological risks. It requires a demonstrated body of work, an existing self-built audience (not necessarily large), a credible distribution plan, monthly updates and a final report. Editorial independence is retained; unspent funding must be returned if the project stops. Payments are often taxable; payment to a company is case-by-case. Restricted jurisdictions and sanctions apply. No lobbying or electoral campaigning. Verify current terms at submission.

## Concrete draft pitch (unsubmitted)
**Working title:** Who Benefits When AI Becomes More Capable?
A six-month, source-linked editorial series of four substantial essays and four short documentary-style explainers on advanced-AI risk, institutional incentives, human agency and the limits of current evidence. Each installment distinguishes measured capabilities, documented incidents, serious expert disagreement and speculative scenarios. The project repurposes existing investigative research while creating original, accessible public-interest journalism. Proposed distribution: the author's verified channels/newsletter/site and partnerships only if confirmed. Impact measurement: actual reach, completion, engagement and reader comprehension; no invented forecasts.

**Illustrative request:** $30,000 over six months: $15,000 author/research time; $6,000 editorial fact-checking; $5,000 video production; $2,000 accessibility/captions; $2,000 distribution/testing. All amounts are placeholders, not quotes or eligible-cost determinations. Avoid overlapping expense claims with any TAIF or Epistemics application.

## Eligibility evidence gate
Before application, obtain links and dated metrics for at least three published works; audience totals and engagement for each relevant channel; copyright/asset permissions; applicant/payee location and sanctions check; exact time commitment; real cost estimates; evidence that editorial work will focus on AI safety rather than generic AI commentary. If audience evidence is absent, deprioritize Media Grants in favor of Rapid Grants.

## Risk-adjusted economics (screening assumptions, NOT grantmaker statistics)
Assume 40% probability of passing eligibility and 5% conditional award probability => 2% joint probability. A $30,000 grant gives $600 expected **gross restricted funding**, not earnings. At 5 hours of preparation × $35/hour, proxy EV is $425. Actual commercial EV = award probability × *permissible retained economic benefit after delivery* minus pursuit cost; presently unknown. A weak audience portfolio can drive probability much lower. Do not pursue more than two hours of additional drafting before evidence is available.
